# Next horizon brief — horizon 21 (after horizon 20: edit-conflict documentation and characterization)

## Recommended scope
Keep horizon 21 small and pick ONE lane, because horizon 20 shipped no behavioural change and the concurrency subject is now closed: the edit-conflict semantics are documented, both branches of the pointer-read window are pinned by unit tests, and the tolerant integration assertion is marked correct-by-design and explicitly not to be tightened — do not re-open any of that, and in particular do not re-add the bounded retry the user deliberately dropped. The highest-value lane is a one-pass audit of blockers.md itself: roughly thirty entries are open, at least one drove an entire wasted horizon after being fixed four horizons earlier, and several (the 200+422 cause, the UI-reachability question, the horizon-17 rollout-replay question) are now answered by horizon 20's findings and can be retired immediately with evidence — that is cheap, mostly documentation, and it protects every future horizon's premise. A plausible alternative lane is one concrete deferred defect with a bounded blast radius, most naturally the orphaned-object cleanup on a lost pointer IfMatch race, but that one touches both s3-snapshot-publisher.ts and s3-segment-publisher.ts and carries real risk to the CLI publish/rollback callers, so it deserves a horizon of its own rather than being bolted onto the audit. Either way, budget two to four phases, expect a unit test for every new packages/*/src branch since integration and e2e contribute zero coverage, and do not combine the backlog audit with a product-code change in the same horizon.
